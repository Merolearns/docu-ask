# Lisbon Travel Guide (SAMPLE DOCUMENT)

This is a short fictional travel guide written as sample data for testing.

## When to go

The best time to visit Lisbon is late spring (May to June) or early autumn
(September to October). Temperatures sit comfortably in the low seventies
Fahrenheit, crowds are thinner than in August, and hotel prices drop noticeably.
Winters are mild but rainy, and many beachside restaurants close from November
through February.

## Getting around

Lisbon's historic Tram 28 rattles through the Alfama and Graça districts and is
worth riding once for the views, but buy the 24-hour transit pass instead of
single tickets — it covers trams, buses, the metro, and the Santa Justa lift.
The metro is the fastest way across town. Taxis and ride-shares are cheap by
European standards for late-night trips back to your hotel.

## What to eat

Start with pastéis de nata, the famous custard tarts, best eaten warm with a
dusting of cinnamon. For dinner, try bacalhau à brás (shredded salt cod with
eggs and potatoes) or grilled sardines in the summer months. Time Out Market
in Cais do Sodré gathers two dozen vendors under one roof and is a good
low-commitment way to sample many dishes. A typical dinner for two with wine
runs about forty to sixty euros away from the main tourist squares.

## Day trips

Sintra is forty minutes by train and holds the colorful Pena Palace — go early
to beat the tour buses. Cascais is a relaxed beach town at the end of the same
train line, good for an afternoon of seafood and a walk along the cliffs to
Boca do Inferno.

## Practical tips

Portugal uses the euro and card payments are accepted almost everywhere, but
carry a few coins for the trams. English is widely spoken in tourist areas.
Pickpockets work the crowded trams and miradouros, so keep valuables zipped
away.
